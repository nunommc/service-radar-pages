# Service Radar — Pitch Deck

*English version · 12 slides · [Open presentation](./pitch-service-radar-en.html)*

## 1. Service Radar

### The intelligent front desk for local services

More bookings. Fewer empty slots. Less admin.

**Discover · Book · Pay · Optimise**

## 2. The problem

### Finding a service is easy. Actually booking it is not.

- **For customers:** language barriers, uncertainty, hidden availability and unanswered messages — especially for travellers and newcomers.
- **For providers:** calls and messages interrupt work; cancellations leave gaps; payments, calendars and customer records live in separate tools.

> The real cost: unused capacity, non-billable admin and customers who drop off before confirming.

## 3. The opportunity

### A simpler experience for both sides

Customers find the right provider, understand the offer and book in their language. Providers turn demand into confirmed work — without managing every step manually.

- **Local residents:** reliable, convenient nearby options.
- **Travellers:** immediate access to services nearby.
- **Expats and newcomers:** less friction in an unfamiliar market.
- **Mobile professionals:** more customers, smarter journeys.

## 4. The solution

### From discovery to completed service. One connected flow.

Service Radar connects customer discovery to provider operations; Frontdesk automates conversations and routine tasks.

**Discover** (location · language) → **Choose** (price · trust) → **Book** (live availability) → **Pay** (deposit · confirmation) → **Return** (recovery · rebooking)

- **Local marketplace:** search by service, location, availability and radius/travel time.
- **Provider workspace:** calendar, payments, customers, policies and performance in one place.
- **Connected automation:** recover capacity and improve utilisation — more than another directory.

## 5. Operational advantage

### A better schedule. Less time driving between clients.

For mobile providers, combining flexible time windows with travel times can create a more efficient working day.

- **Fixed appointments — conceptual example:** Tychy → Bieruń → Mikołów, with potentially inefficient travel.
- **With flexible windows — proposal:** the system suggests a better sequence; customer and provider approve.

*Conceptual illustration, not measured savings. Real optimisation considers time windows, service duration, traffic and provider rules.*

## 6. Revenue recovery

### A cancellation doesn't have to mean lost revenue

When a slot opens up, the platform can find relevant customers on a waitlist and present an offer approved by the provider.

1. **Detect:** identify the opening and services that fit.
2. **Offer:** contact only customers with appropriate consent.
3. **Confirm:** keep payment, calendar and policies in sync.
4. **Measure:** compare incremental revenue and discount.

## 7. Frontdesk

### A multilingual front desk. Connected to the live calendar.

A web and WhatsApp assistant, with voice as a later option. It answers service and availability questions and acts within configured rules.

- **Answers:** price, duration, location and policies.
- **Books:** validated bookings, changes and cancellations.
- **Escalates:** sensitive or unusual cases to a person.
- **Stays reliable:** no invented availability; transparent AI disclosure.

**Example conversation**

> **Customer:** “Do you have a massage this evening?”  
> **Frontdesk:** “Yes. We offer a 60-minute relaxing massage, a 60-minute sports massage, or a 90-minute deep-tissue massage. Which would you prefer?”  
> **Customer:** “How much is the relaxing massage?”  
> **Frontdesk:** “It's €55. I have 5:30 or 6:15 pm available. Would you prefer the studio or a home visit?”

The system checks configured prices and the live calendar; after the customer chooses, it shows the deposit and policy, then confirms after payment.

## 8. Differentiation

### Online booking isn't enough. The edge is the complete workflow.

| Capability | Booksy / Fresha / Goldie | Service Radar — proposed focus |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery + booking | Established offering | Multilingual; availability and location-led |
| Deposits and payments | Available across several products | Connected to policies, recovery and reconciliation |
| AI assistant | Already present in some products | Interface for validated actions, not an autonomous promise |
| Mobile provider | Validate through competitor research | Flexible windows + route optimisation as a wedge |
| Revenue recovery | Waitlist features vary | Close the loop: cancellation → offer → payment → calendar |

*Strategic view based on the business plan, not an exhaustive feature audit. Verify through mystery-shopping before making public claims.*

## 9. Business model

### Subscription + commission. More value, lower take rate.

| Plan | Includes | Indicative price and commission |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | Profile, calendar and bookings | €0/month · 8% |
| Growth | Recovery, team tools and analytics | €69/month · 3.5% |
| Frontdesk AI | Assistant, routes and automation | €149/month · 2% |

Providers pay for measurable outcomes: completed bookings, recovered slots and less admin time.

*Illustrative test prices, not final pricing. Payment processing, AI usage and operating costs are excluded.*

## 10. Go to market

### Start focused. Build density before scaling.

Proposal: pilot in one cluster, starting with mobile beauty and wellness professionals; test cancellation recovery with a small group of fixed-location salons.

- **Supply first:** recruit 15–25 providers as a pilot hypothesis.
- **Local demand:** activate provider audiences and local discovery.
- **Example geography:** Bieruń–Tychy–Mikołów to test routes; validate density.
- **Expand on evidence:** Poland → Portugal → UK, if supply supports it.

## 11. Plan and validation

### Prove value before accelerating

1. **Discover:** interview providers and customers; choose segment, city and PSP.
2. **Paid bookings:** profiles, availability, bookings, deposits and reconciled payments.
3. **Recovery:** waitlist, consented offers and incremental revenue measurement.
4. **Intelligence:** flexible routes and Frontdesk; expand after ROI evidence.

**Metrics to track:**

- Marketplace: conversion to completed booking, repeat use and useful search results.
- Provider economics: revenue per available hour, recovered cancellations and admin time.
- Operations: travel time, proposal acceptance, refunds and incidents.

*Product status, traction, team and funding requirement were not documented in the supplied sources and are not assumed here.*
